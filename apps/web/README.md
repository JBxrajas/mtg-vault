# apps/web

SPA built with Vite + React + TanStack Router and Query.

It must remain a pure SPA (no SSR) so that `apps/desktop` can load the same build. See [ADR 0002](../../docs/adr/0002-spa-for-electron-reuse.md).

- Assets use relative paths (`base: "./"`) and routing uses hash history, so the build also works from `file://` in Electron.
- The UI is in English and Spanish ([ADR 0010](../../docs/adr/0010-interface-languages.md)); see **Languages** below.

## Scripts

```bash
pnpm --filter @mtg-vault/web dev        # dev server on http://localhost:5173
pnpm --filter @mtg-vault/web build      # typecheck and production build into dist/
pnpm --filter @mtg-vault/web typecheck
```

## Design

Mobile first. One stylesheet (`src/styles.css`) with design tokens as CSS variables: a light palette by default and a dark one that follows the system (`prefers-color-scheme`). The frame is `src/Layout.tsx`: brand and account in a top bar, and the main navigation as a **bottom tab bar on phones** and inline in the top bar from 720 px up. Search results are a card-image grid (2 columns on a phone, more on wide screens); the card dialog is a full-height bottom sheet on phones and a centered modal on wide screens; buttons and fields are at least 44 px tall for touch. Mana costs are drawn as coloured pips by `src/ManaCost.tsx`, and the colour filter uses the same colours. Do not put `backdrop-filter` on the top bar: it makes the fixed bottom navigation position itself relative to the bar instead of the screen.

The first version of this design was checked with screenshots from headless Chromium (390×844 phone and 1280×800 desktop, light and dark) of search, the card dialog, the collection and the sign-in page. A script that opens every view at 320, 360, 390, 430, 768 and 1280 px and compares `scrollWidth` with the viewport found one real overflow (the add-card row on the deck page, an input without `min-width: 0`), now fixed; after that no view overflows horizontally. That script is not part of the test suite. Real devices and browsers other than Chromium have not been tried.

## Onboarding and help

The **landing page** (`src/LandingPage.tsx`, styles in `src/landing.css`) is the front door: `#/` shows it to someone who is not signed in and the card search to someone who is (`src/Home.tsx`; it shows neither while it is still finding out who is there, so the wrong page does not flash). It also lives at `#/welcome` for anyone, to share, and the card search is at `#/search` (the top bar's *Search* link). It has a hero with a fan of real card pictures (hotlinked from Scryfall, like the rest of the app), four facts, eight features, how it works in four steps, three deep dives with sample screens (scanner, deck page, collection search) built in HTML and CSS rather than as screenshots, so they stay sharp, follow the theme and are translated, a short FAQ and a closing call to action. The sample screens are labelled *Example* and hidden from screen readers. A signed-in visitor sees *Open my collection* instead of *Create your account*. The text is English and Spanish (`landing.*`). Every claim in it comes from this repository: the figures (55,000+ illustrations, 120,000+ printings, four scan languages) are the index and the catalog, and the scanner's accuracy is given as the simulation result with its limits, not as a promise. It does not say the service is free, has price history or has an app, because none of that is decided or built. Checked in headless Chromium at 360, 390 and 1280 px in both languages (nothing wider than the window); not reviewed by a designer or tried on a real phone.
Visitors who are not signed in see a **welcome panel** on the search page (`src/Welcome.tsx`: what an account is for, with *Create a free account* and *Sign in*), and *My collection* and *My decks* show a short pitch with the same button instead of a bare "sign in" line. *Create a free account* links to `#/login?mode=register`, which opens the form on account creation (the `mode` search param is validated in `src/router.tsx`). The nav links and the header *Sign in* button have native `title` hints.

Basic terms have a **help bubble** (`src/Tip.tsx`, a small "?" button): search and its filters, finish / condition / quantity in the add form, stacks and market value in the collection, the deck format and the deck boards. It opens on hover, keyboard focus-and-Enter or tap (a plain `title` never shows on phones), closes with Escape, a tap elsewhere or when the mouse leaves, is announced through `role="tooltip"` and `aria-describedby`, and is shifted back inside the screen when it would be clipped. Put a `Tip` next to a label, never inside a `<label>`: a button inside a label takes over the field's association. Nor inside a box that scrolls (`overflow: auto`): the box clips the bubble and scrolls to it, as happened with the deck tabs, which scroll sideways on a phone; put the `Tip` beside the box. Checked in headless Chromium at 390 px and 1100 px (no horizontal overflow, bubbles inside the screen); not tried on a real phone.

### Sign-in and sign-up errors

`src/LoginPage.tsx` checks the whole form at once and shows each message **under its own field** (red border, `aria-invalid`, linked with `aria-describedby`); fixing a field clears only its error. The rules are always visible under the fields, the page says that no email is needed, and an `@` in the username gets "Use a username, not an email". A taken username from the server is shown under the username field; other server errors (for example a wrong password) stay as one message above the button. The messages for the username and password rules live in `packages/shared` (`usernameSchema`, `passwordSchema`).

### Search filters

The type filter lists the common card types as chips (Creature, Instant, Sorcery, Artifact, Enchantment, Planeswalker, Land, Battle; tap again to clear) and still accepts any word of the type line (goblin, equipment, legendary). The color buttons show the color name under each pip and a sentence saying what is being filtered, for example "Cards that have all of: Red and Green." With several colors a card must have all of them (`color_identity @> …` in the API); picking none returns every color, including lands and colorless cards.

### Session and cached data

The session token lives in `localStorage` (`src/auth.ts`), but React Query also caches what the API returned (collection, decks…). `refreshSession` in `src/session.ts` runs after every sign-in, account creation and sign-out: it drops every cached query that is not public (the card search stays) and re-reads who is signed in. Without it, signing out left the previous collection on screen, and the next person to sign in would briefly see it. *My collection* and *My decks* also show nothing from the cache when there is no signed-in user. Any new account-bound query key must not start with `cards` or `me`.

### Quantity buttons

`+` and `−` on *My collection* are **optimistic** (`src/CollectionPage.tsx`): the number and the totals change on tap and the request follows. Requests of the same list run one after another (mutation `scope`), so quick taps cannot reach the server out of order, and the buttons stay enabled meanwhile. When the server refuses a change (for example copies placed in binders) the old number comes back with the message. The list is re-read once, after the last request ends, instead of after every tap.

## Languages

English is the source and the fallback; Spanish is a translation. Every UI message is a key in `src/messages/en.ts` with its Spanish text in `src/messages/es.ts`, and the build fails if a key is missing in Spanish. Use `const { t } = useI18n()` in components (they re-render when the language changes) and `t(key, vars)` elsewhere: `{name}` placeholders, `{ one, other }` for plurals (the key takes `count`). The start language is the saved choice (`localStorage` `mtg-vault.locale`), else the browser's, else English; the **EN / ES button** in the top bar and the *Language* section of *Appearance* change it, and `<html lang>` follows. Prices use the language's number format. Card names and type lines are English data from Scryfall and are not translated, so the type filter still sends English words; errors that come from the server are shown as received, except the sign-in and sign-up ones we know (`serverMessage` in `src/LoginPage.tsx`). Tests start in English whatever the machine's language (`test-setup.ts`); `i18n.test.ts` checks that both catalogs have the same keys, placeholders and plural shape, and `i18n-pages.test.tsx` renders screens in Spanish. Why this and not Lingui: [ADR 0010](../../docs/adr/0010-interface-languages.md).

## Collection search, sorting and filters

The collection page (`src/CollectionFilters.tsx`, logic in `packages/shared/src/collection-filter.ts`) has one **toolbar** (search, sort with a direction button, a **List / Cards / Art** switch and a **Filters** button with a badge) and, always in view under it, **card types as chips with how many stacks each has** (All, Creature, Instant, Land…; types the collection has none of are left out), **colors** (including colorless) and **mana value** (0 to 7+, several at once). The **Filters** button opens a panel with rarity, finish, condition and set. Everything that is on is shown as a chip with an ✕ under the panel, with a *Clear filters* link, so nothing narrows the list out of sight. The **search box** needs every word in the name, type line, set name or code, collector number or notes (accents and case ignored); **sorting** is by name, set, copies, price each, total value or mana value, ascending or descending. A card's type is its main type (an Artifact Creature is a Creature). Inside one filter any choice matches, except colors: a card must have every color picked (Red + Green finds red-green cards, more colors allowed), and colorless is exclusive with the others; the filters add up. While any filter is set the page says how many stacks, cards and how much value are shown out of the whole. Filtering happens in the browser over the stacks the page already loads, so it is instant; the totals at the top and the CSV export always cover the whole collection. The filters are not remembered between visits. The three views show the same filtered stacks: a row per stack, a grid of card pictures (`.card-grid`), or the art gallery. (Before this, the filters sat in a folded panel whose layout clashed with the card search's `.filters` style and looked broken; the class is no longer shared.) Checked in headless Chromium at 360, 390 and 1280 px (e2e); not tried on a real phone.

## Prices in pesos, and high-resolution art

- **Pesos** (`src/currency.ts`, `src/CurrencyControl.tsx`): the collection can show prices in US dollars (as Scryfall gives them) or Mexican pesos, converted with a rate the user types (1 USD = N MXN, between 0.01 and 1000). The rate starts at 18 and is a guess, not a live rate; there is no call to an exchange-rate service. It is saved on the device. Sorting by price each or total value works the same in either currency.
- **High-resolution art** (`src/ArtPage.tsx`, `src/art.ts`, route `#/art/:printingId`): each stack in the collection has a "High-resolution art" link that opens in a new window, and the collection has an **Art** view, a gallery of the art of what the filters leave. The page shows the art at the largest size Scryfall serves (626 × 457 px; the full card is 745 × 1040 px, with a switch between them), the artist with a link to more of their art on Scryfall, the printing, flavor text and a link to the card on Scryfall. The artist is not in our database, so the page asks Scryfall for the printing when it is opened. Image and page addresses from the answer are accepted only if they are Scryfall's. The appearance page links to this view for the art chosen as the background once a printing is picked (`printingId` is saved with the art).
- The page **background** keeps using the art-only image (`art_crop`, 626 × 457 px) because Scryfall has nothing larger of the art alone; on a big screen it is stretched, which is why it looks soft.

## Card scanner

Route `#/scan` ("Scan" in the navigation), for signed-in users. Design and measurements in [ADR 0014](../../docs/adr/0014-card-scanning-with-art-fingerprints.md). Everything happens on the device: a photo is never uploaded or stored.

- **Card data download** (`src/scan/artIndex.ts`): on opening, the art-fingerprint index (about 3.6 MB, `GET /art-index`) is downloaded with a progress bar and kept in IndexedDB; a copy younger than 7 days is used without asking the server, and an older copy is used if the download fails. Without IndexedDB (private windows) it is downloaded each time.
- **Camera** (`src/ScanCamera.tsx`): rear camera when there is one, with a card-shaped guide. The guide goes from **red to green** with the focus of what is inside it (variance of the Laplacian, `src/scan/sharpness.ts`, read four times a second); a card is captured by itself when the view **holds still** (the art fingerprint of the guide changes by at most 28 bits between readings), is **sharp enough** (focus level 0.6 or more, a little below the green mark) and is not a flat picture, for three readings in a row (`AutoCapture`, unit tested). The checkbox turns that off and the button always works. The same card is not captured twice while it stays in view; the next capture is armed as soon as the view differs by 64 bits or more from the card taken (another card, or the card taken away), so cards can be shown one after another without touching the phone and without the focus having to drop in between (a textured table never lets it drop). A card that was not recognised is not retried until the view changes. A capture does not look at one place only: it searches for the card around the guide (the guide, then 10 % smaller and bigger, shifted by steps; `src/scan/search.ts`, unit tested), because the fingerprint of a card a little off the guide is quite different. In a simulation that took a typical shot from 16 % to 87 % recognised ([ADR 0014](../../docs/adr/0014-card-scanning-with-art-fingerprints.md)); not measured on a real phone. A short beep confirms a capture (a low one means nothing was recognised).
- **Photos**: *Take a photo* (opens the phone's camera app) and *Choose photos* (several at once). Without a guide, the app tries the card filling the picture, at the size of the guide and smaller, each as it is and turned a quarter turn either way, and keeps the closest match (`matchPhoto` in `src/scan/capture.ts`).
- **Recognising**: the art inside the guide is shrunk with `grayGridFromRgba` (the same function the index job uses), hashed and compared with the index (`src/scan/identify.ts`). A flat picture is never matched. The best match is **sure**, **maybe** (the list says so and offers the other candidate cards) or **none**.
- **Toast**: card name and price (in the currency chosen on the collection page) in a `role="status"` region for a few seconds. Scanning the same card again raises its quantity instead of adding a row.
- **Printing**: the photo identifies the art, not the printing, so the list suggests the newest printing in the **language chosen** (English, Spanish, Japanese, Russian, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Korean, Chinese; remembered on the device), else the newest English one, and each row has a printing, finish and quantity to change before adding.
- **Batch and decks**: the list collects what was scanned; *Add N cards* sends it to **My collection** or to one of the user's decks (the *Add to* selector), one request per card, keeping any card that failed with its reason. A deck is filled one card at a time the same way, so a whole deck can be scanned in one session.

Tested in jsdom (`ScanPage.test.tsx`, `src/scan/*.test.ts`) and in Chromium with a fake camera and a generated card picture (`e2e/scan.spec.ts`: recognised from a photo and added, a flat picture says no card, the guide and meter on a real video frame).

## Deck and collection import and export

- **Collection**: an *Export CSV* button on *My collection* downloads the stacks in the layout the import reads (`collectionToCsv`, with a byte-order mark so Excel reads accents).
- **Decks**: *My decks* has an *Import a deck* panel (name, format, paste a list or choose a `.txt`; the name is taken from the file name when empty) that creates the deck and imports the list; with nothing to report it opens the deck, otherwise it stays to show the cards that were not found. A deck page has an *Import / export list* panel: *Copy list*, *Download .txt* (named after the deck) and an import into that deck. The count in the list sets the copies, so importing the same list twice changes nothing. The list format and what was tested are in [docs/apis.md](../../docs/apis.md#deck-lists-text-import-and-export); the endpoint is in [apps/api](../api/README.md).
- Reading and writing the formats (`parseDeckList`, `formatDeckList`, `collectionToCsv`, `toCsv`) is in `packages/shared`; `src/download.ts` hands a file to the browser; `src/DeckListInput.tsx` is the text box with preview, `src/DeckListTools.tsx` and `src/ImportDeck.tsx` the two panels.
- Messages that come back from the server about a list (for example "No card found") are English.

## Appearance

The palette icon in the top bar opens `#/appearance` (`src/AppearancePage.tsx`): **mode** (automatic, light or dark), a **background color** (a tint named after the five Magic colors, plus gold and neutral, mixed into the neutral background of the current mode with `color-mix`) or the **art of any card** found by name, with a *Dimming* slider (50 to 95 %) for how much the page color covers the art. The choice applies at once and is saved **on this device only** in `localStorage` (`src/appearance.ts`: parsing, validation, `setAppearance`, `useAppearance`); it is applied before the first render to avoid a flash. Mechanically it sets `data-theme`, `data-bg` and `data-art` on `<html>` and the `--art-url` / `--art-scrim` variables that `src/styles.css` reads; the art is a fixed `body::before` layer covered by a `body::after` layer in the page color.

After choosing a card, a *Printing* section lists its other printings by art (printings that share a picture appear once, under the first) so a different artwork can be picked; the card's id is kept with the choice for that (`oracleId` in `Art`, only accepted when it is a UUID). The art URL ends up inside CSS, so only `https://cards.scryfall.io/…` addresses made of safe characters are accepted, both when picking and when reading what was stored (`isArtUrl`). Not done yet: saving the choice in the account so it follows the user across devices, and showing the artist's name for the chosen art.

## Commander-first deck flow

A Commander deck starts with its commander, because everything else is checked against it.
- **Guided start** (`src/NewCommanderDeck.tsx`, on the decks page): name, then commander, then "the 99" (a pasted text list, as Moxfield exports it, or "I'll choose them later"). The deck is created with the commander on the commander board and the chosen printing saved on it. A list that repeats the commander does not add it twice (`dropCommanderRows` in `packages/shared`).
- **Commander chooser** (`src/CommanderChooser.tsx`, also on a Commander deck that has no commander): a search limited to legendary cards, a grid of images (cards that cannot lead are greyed out and say why: not legendary, not a creature, cannot partner), then the chosen card large with every printing to pick its art from. Rules in `packages/shared/src/commander.ts`: legendary creature, "can be your commander" text, Backgrounds, and Partner / Partner with / Friends forever / Choose a Background / Doctor's companion pairs. Legendary Vehicles and Spacecraft count only when their text says so (we do not hold power and toughness).
- **Order enforced**: on a Commander deck with no commander, the list import waits ("Choose the commander first"); adding single cards is still possible. A second commander can be added from the bar when the first can have a partner.
- **Colors**: the identity shows as pips and as its common name (Temur, Azorius, Five-color…).
- **Problems highlighted** on the card itself (red border and a note): outside the commander's colors (with the colors), repeated copies, the commander also in the main board, a commander that cannot lead (`cardProblems` in `packages/shared`).
- **Groups fold** (button with `aria-expanded`, "Collapse all" / "Expand all"); a folded group still says how many of its cards have a problem.
- **Bigger card**: hovering a thumbnail on a computer floats the large image beside it; on a phone an *Enlarge* button under the image opens it in a dialog (`src/CardPreview.tsx`). The button also works by keyboard. The same thumbnail is used in the deck lists, the deck's add-card results and the collection list; opening the dialog closes the hover image so they never show together.

## Commander analysis

The **decks list** (`src/DecksPage.tsx`) shows each deck as a card with its **commander's art as the background** (darkened so the text reads on any art), the format, the commander's colors and a **bar for the deck size** (green at the format's size, red above it; formats without a size have no bar). `GET /decks` sends the commander (`DeckSummary.commander`: name, colors, picture). A deck page opens with a **header** (`src/DeckHero.tsx`) that uses the same art, the deck size bar, a **bar of how the cards divide among types** with a legend and, when there are any, how many cards have a rule warning. On a screen 1000 px wide or more the **analysis is a side column** that stays in view while the cards scroll; on a phone it starts folded (`src/FoldOnPhone.tsx`) so the cards are not pushed down. Settings and Import / export sit side by side. A Commander deck page keeps the **commander in a bar** under the top bar (`src/CommanderBar.tsx`: artwork, name, color identity; sticky while the page scrolls, whichever board tab is open; it says so when no commander is chosen yet). It also shows an analysis panel (`src/DeckAnalysis.tsx`, only when the deck's format is Commander): the commander and its color identity, **lands against the suggested count**, average mana value, copies of **ramp, card draw, removal and board wipes** next to their usual range, a **mana curve** (bars from 0 to 7+ with a text label per bar), **warnings**, a **brackets guide** and the list of sources with how much to trust each. Warnings come in two kinds, *Format rules* (100 cards, one or two commanders, colors inside the commander's identity, one copy of each card) and *Suggestions* (lands and role counts outside the usual range, only once the deck has 60 cards); none of them blocks anything. The figures, their sources and what was and was not verified are in [docs/knowledge/commander.md](../../docs/knowledge/commander.md); the decision is [ADR 0013](../../docs/adr/0013-commander-knowledge-and-analysis.md). The calculation is `analyzeCommanderDeck` in `packages/shared` (roles are guessed from the card text, so they are estimates).

**EDHREC** is linked, not used as data. Each commander gets a link to its EDHREC page (new tab, `rel="noopener noreferrer"`) and a *Side window* button that opens the same page in a small window (`src/edhrec.ts`, `window.open` with `popup,noopener,noreferrer`). EDHREC refuses to be embedded in a page and its terms forbid automated access, so nothing is fetched, proxied or copied. The page slug rule was checked against one commander.

Tests: the panel (counts, links, side window, curve labels, warnings, brackets, sources, Spanish) and the slug and window helpers; the analysis itself is tested in `packages/shared`. Layout is checked in a real browser (see `e2e/` in the repository root, including a sweep that opens the main screens at 360 and 390 px in English and Spanish and fails if anything is wider than the window: sticky bar, hover preview, enlarge button, folded groups, the guided start, no horizontal overflow, phone and desktop width).

## Tests

`pnpm test` from the repository root. Pure logic (`api.ts`, `auth.ts`, `collectionApi.ts`, `format.ts`) is tested in Node. **Components** (`*.test.tsx`) run in jsdom with Testing Library and `userEvent`: the file starts with `// @vitest-environment jsdom`, the API modules (`./api`, `./auth`, `./collectionApi`) are mocked with `vi.mock`, and `renderPage` in `src/test-utils.tsx` renders a page inside a query client and an in-memory router (other routes render a `route: <path>` marker so tests can assert a navigation). The same file has builders (`aCard`, `aPrinting`, `aStack`, `aUser`). They cover the help bubble (`Tip`), the welcome panel, the search (debounce, filters, empty and error states, the printings dialog), sign in and account creation (validation, server errors, redirect), the header, the add-to-collection form and the collection page (totals, quantity, removal, refused changes). Query by role or label like a user would, not by CSS class. What they cannot show is layout and styling: that still needs eyes on a browser.

## Current state

The home page is a card search: a name box (debounced, 2 characters minimum), a type filter and color-identity toggles, with a card thumbnail per result (click it to open a dialog with the card and **every printing**: set name and code, collector number, year, rarity, finishes and the latest price; click a printing to see its image), backed by `GET /cards/search` ([apps/api](../api/README.md)). The fetch layer is in `src/api.ts` (unit tested); the page is `src/SearchPage.tsx`. Every screen has component tests (see **Tests** below); nothing here has been checked in a real browser by automation. Images are hotlinked from Scryfall with lazy loading and the page shows the credit Scryfall asks for. A **Sign in / Create account** screen (`#/login`) uses a username and a password; the session token is kept in `localStorage` and the header shows who is signed in (`src/auth.ts`, unit tested). Opening a card shows an **Add to collection** form for the selected printing (finish limited to what that printing offers, condition, quantity) when signed in. **My collection** (`#/collection`) lists your stacks with image, set, finish, condition, market price and line total, a header with copies, stacks and total market value (copies without a price are counted separately), `+` / `−` to change the quantity and Remove (`src/collectionApi.ts`, unit tested). **Import from CSV** (a panel at the top of *My collection*, `src/ImportCollection.tsx`): choose a CSV exported from Moxfield, see how many lines and cards it holds, which lines cannot be read and which columns are ignored, then import. It reports how many stacks were created, updated or already up to date, and lists the lines imported with a change and the ones that could not be imported. Large files are sent in chunks with a progress count. The count in the file sets the quantity of each stack, so importing the same file again changes nothing. **My decks** (`#/decks`) lists decks with format and progress towards the format's size, and creates a new one. Opening a deck (`#/decks/<id>`) shows its main board **divided by card type** (Commander, Planeswalker, Creature, Sorcery, Instant, Artifact, Enchantment, Battle, Land) with a count per group, tabs for sideboard and maybeboard, a quantity stepper per card, an *Owned / Own 1/4* badge from your collection, a search box to add cards to a chosen board (adding one closes the results, clears the box, keeps the focus in it and says what was added in a line below, so the next card can be typed straight away) (the commander board only for Commander, Brawl and Oathbreaker) and a settings panel (name, format, public, delete with confirmation) (`src/DecksPage.tsx`, `src/DeckPage.tsx`, `src/decksApi.ts`). Binders come later (see [docs/plan.md](../../docs/plan.md)).
